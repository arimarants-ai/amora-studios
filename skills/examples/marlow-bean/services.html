Grooming

What we do, and what it actually costs.

Every dog is different, so a fixed price list would be a guess. Here is what each service involves and what moves the number, then tell us about your dog and we will give you a real figure.

Full groom

A bath, a full haircut to the style you want, ears, nails and a blow dry that actually finishes.

Around two and a half hours for most dogs.

Bath and tidy

A wash and blow dry with the face, feet and sanitary areas tidied up. No full haircut.

Good between full grooms, or for short coats.

Puppy's first groom

A short, gentle session that teaches a puppy that the table, the clippers and the dryer are nothing to worry about.

For dogs under six months. Half price.

De-shedding

A bath with a de-shedding treatment and a thorough undercoat rake, for double coated breeds losing their winter.

Huskies, shepherds, retrievers, anything leaving hair on the sofa.

Nails and paws

Nail trim, pad shave and paw balm. Ten minutes, no appointment needed on weekday mornings.

Walk in Tuesday to Friday before noon.

Pricing

Three things move the price.

Size. A cocker spaniel takes half the time a bernese does. This is most of it.

Coat. A curly or double coat is slower than a short one, at every stage.

Condition. A coat brushed at home between visits is quick. A matted one is not, and that is the difference between $65 and $140 on the same dog.

A full groom runs $65 to $140. Bath and tidy is $40 to $75. Puppy grooms are half the full groom price. Nails on their own are $15. We confirm your price when you book.

Questions

The ones we get asked.

What does it cost?

A full groom for a small dog in good condition starts at $65, and a large double coated dog in a bad state can reach $140. Size, coat type and how matted it is are what move the number. We confirm the price when you book, not after.

My dog is matted. Will you shave them?

Sometimes, and we will tell you before we start. Brushing out a badly matted coat hurts, and a short coat that grows back is kinder than an hour of pulling. We would rather have that conversation than surprise you.

My dog has bitten a groomer before.

Tell us. It does not automatically mean no. It means we start with a look round, then a short session, and we build up. What we cannot do is find out halfway through a full groom.

Do you take cats?

No. Dogs only.

How often should we come?

Most coats want six to eight weeks. Doodles and other curly coats closer to four, or the matting gets ahead of you. Short coats can go three months.
